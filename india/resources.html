India: resources, referral and government pathways

India needs a resource page that is useful without pretending to be a substitute for the healthcare system. First Signs maintains a dated directory of verified resources rather than an unranked list of names.

On this page
  • Government programmes and schemes
  • Indian medical resources
  • Hindi-language information
  • International references and support organisations
  • Our objective is navigation
Key point
Our promise on accuracy: this page will never state that a programme covers a particular craniosynostosis case unless that coverage has been verified. Where we could not verify something, we say exactly that, and we tell you what to ask instead. Resources change, so every entry carries a verification date.

Government programmes and schemes

One honest sentence first: no government pathway we searched in September 2026 names craniosynostosis. Craniosynostosis is not among the nine birth defects named under RBSK, and we could not verify a named PM-JAY package in the official documents we searched. Coverage can still be possible under general neurosurgery packages, which is why each entry below tells you exactly what to ask.

RBSK (Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram)

The national child health screening and early intervention programme (birth to 18 years). Its nine named birth defects do not include craniosynostosis, so there is no automatic pathway; families can still ask their District Early Intervention Centre (DEIC) how a suspected skull condition is referred locally.

What to ask: Ask your ASHA worker, anganwadi or DEIC: how is a suspected skull or head-shape condition referred under RBSK in this district?

Verified 25 September 2026 · Official link

Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY

The national health assurance scheme for eligible families. We could not verify a craniosynostosis-named package in the HBP 2.2 guidelines document or a state package master we searched; a third-party blog claims coverage but without a package code. Coverage may still be possible under general neurosurgery packages at an empanelled hospital.

What to ask: At an empanelled hospital, ask the Ayushman Mitra desk to search the current package list in front of you, ask which specialty package the operation would be filed under, and request the answer in writing.

Verified 25 September 2026 · Official link

MJPJAY (Maharashtra)

Mahatma Jyotirao Phule Jan Arogya Yojana, Maharashtra's state scheme, roughly 2,399 packages after the 2023 expansion. A craniosynostosis-named package was not found in our search; ask the scheme desk at an empanelled hospital to check the current list.

What to ask: Ask the Arogyamitra at an empanelled Maharashtra hospital to check the live package list for the named procedure your surgeon writes down.

Verified 25 September 2026 · Official link

CGHS

Central Government Health Scheme, for central government employees and pensioners. Rate lists include neurosurgical procedures such as cranioplasty in older city lists; a craniosynostosis-specific package was not found in our search.

What to ask: If your family is CGHS-covered, ask your wellness centre which listed neurosurgical procedure code the treating hospital would use.

Verified 25 September 2026 · Official link

NHM Birth Defects Handbook

The National Health Mission's screening handbook for birth defects, useful background on how India's screening system is organised.

Verified 25 September 2026 · Official link
