Recognise the first signs

Why First Signs exists

We are building an India-focused, medically governed information and family-navigation platform, beginning with craniosynostosis. Our purpose is not to diagnose a child online. Our purpose is to help families understand the condition, recognise what questions to ask, find the appropriate specialist pathway and navigate the information around diagnosis, treatment and follow-up.

Published international estimates place craniosynostosis at roughly 1 in 1,800 to 1 in 2,500 births, and a 2025 Indian tertiary-centre series found children presenting at an average age of about 10.9 months, often past the window in which the least invasive treatment is generally considered. Early recognition changes what is possible. That is the whole reason for this platform's name.

Most importantly: a website cannot examine your baby. If you are concerned about your child's head shape, development, vision, hearing, breathing or other symptoms, speak with a qualified healthcare professional. First Signs is here to help you arrive at that conversation better informed, not to replace it.

A community, planned for a later phase

In our September 2026 research we could not find a dedicated Indian craniosynostosis parent community in our searches of the major platforms; Indian parents were posting into groups run from other countries. A moderated First Signs community, for lived experience shared between families with medical questions answered only by clinicians, is planned as a future activity.
