About First Signs and the DAF partnership

First Signs is an India-focused, medically governed information and family-navigation platform, beginning with craniosynostosis and built to widen to other rare pre-birth and birth defects.

Our mission

No family in India should face craniosynostosis without clear, trustworthy information.

First Signs helps families recognise the first signs early, understand the condition in plain language, find the right specialist pathway, prepare for every stage of treatment, and know that they are not alone. We do this with medical review on every clinical page and lived experience clearly labelled, and we never diagnose a child online.

How First Signs began

Vedaansh Agarwal was born with left unilateral coronal craniosynostosis. His parents first noticed that one eyelid did not fully close during sleep, and the family saw several specialties before the condition was correctly identified. At 14 months he had corrective surgery in Dallas, performed by Dr. Jeffrey Fearon.

Years later, Vedaansh looked at what an Indian family would find today if they searched the way his parents once did. Families in the US and UK are given parent groups, surgery guides, specialist directories and helplines. In India, our searches found almost none of this, and published Indian research shows children here are often first seen late, at an average of about 10.9 months. First Signs was started to close that gap. The Debabrata Mitalee Auro Foundation agreed to take it forward together at its Board meeting on 21 September 2026.

What our research found

Before writing a single page, we audited 13 international support organisations, 13 major hospital and public health websites, and the Indian landscape of hospitals, professional societies, communities, government schemes, insurance and regional-language content (25 September 2026). Where something was not found, we name the searches we ran. We never say something does not exist without checking.

How we work: medical governance

  • No diagnosis online. No "photo diagnosis" tool, ever. A website cannot examine a baby.
  • No rankings. The doctor directory exists for navigation, not endorsement.
  • Doctors listed only after formal agreement. No doctor or specialist is named as part of First Signs until they have formally agreed to take part.
  • Medical approval: each clinical page is approved by the relevant specialist and the Foundation's designated medical representative before it goes live.
  • Review records on every clinical page: a named medical reviewer, review date, version number and source list. A change after approval goes back to the relevant reviewers.
  • Verification dates everywhere: every directory entry and resource carries the date we verified it.
  • Attributed honesty: where respected centres publish different numbers (surgery timing, transfusion rates, helmet duration), we present the range with each centre named, never a single number pretending to be consensus.
  • Lived experience is labelled. Family stories are kept separate from medical content.
  • International review: Dr. Jeffrey Fearon (The Craniofacial Center, Dallas) has agreed to review our craniosynostosis content.

What First Signs is, and is not

First Signs is

  • Educational information in plain language
  • A navigation guide to specialists, schemes and costs in India
  • A moderated family community, planned for a later phase
  • A partnership between lived experience and medical expertise

First Signs is not

  • A diagnostic service
  • A substitute for a consultation
  • A ranking of doctors or hospitals
  • A place for individual treatment advice

Where we are going

First Signs begins with craniosynostosis, as its own independent website, developed in partnership with the Debabrata Mitalee Auro Foundation. Later steps, such as an initial educational video, outreach to hospitals and doctor networks, a family community and reviewed Hindi and Marathi versions, each go ahead only with the Foundation Board's written approval. The structure is built so other rare birth defect sections can be added later without a rebuild.
