Your journey

Long-term follow-up and development

For most families, the milestones after surgery slowly stop being medical and start being ordinary: growth, school, sport, personality. Follow-up exists to keep it that way.

←→
Long-term follow-up and growing up

What long-term care can involve

  • Growth monitoring: the team may review head growth and shape at intervals they set, commonly over months and then years.
  • Development: paediatric reviews may look at milestones, learning and behaviour, so that any need for support is picked up early.
  • Vision and hearing: some children have scheduled eye and hearing checks; ask whether your child needs them and when.
  • Dental and facial growth: for some types, especially syndromic forms, dental and midface growth reviews may be part of the plan.
  • Rarely, further treatment: a small number of children need additional procedures later. Ask your team what they watch for and how they would know.

Questions that keep follow-up useful

  • What is the follow-up schedule for the next two years, written down?
  • What exactly is being checked at each visit?
  • What should we watch for between visits, and who do we call?
  • Can routine reviews be coordinated with a doctor in our own city, with the surgical team reviewing remotely?
  • How will we monitor development, and are any assessments recommended?
  • What records should we maintain as our child grows?
Key point
For travelling families: ask whether some follow-up can happen through photographs, video visits or a local paediatrician sharing findings with the surgical team. Many centres now support remote review for stable, routine checks; whether it fits your child is your team's call.

Children grow up. A question many parents ask years later is how care continues into the teenage years and adulthood, particularly for syndromic conditions. Ask your team when, if ever, your child should be reviewed again as they grow, and keep the surgical records safely; your child may want them one day.
