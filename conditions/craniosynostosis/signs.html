Signs and when to ask for help

Babies' heads can have many normal variations. A head shape that concerns you does not automatically mean craniosynostosis.

Learn · 2 of 11
←→
Signs and when to ask for help

Parents may notice a head that appears unusually long and narrow, a triangular or pointed forehead, flattening or asymmetry, or an unusual change in the shape of the forehead or back of the head. A clinician may also notice changes in the fontanelle, skull contour or head growth.

The purpose of this page is not to give parents a checklist for self-diagnosis. It is to help you decide when to start a medical conversation.

If you are concerned, document these things

  • Take clear photographs over time, if appropriate
  • Note when you first noticed the change
  • Record whether the shape appears to be changing
  • Bring your child's growth and medical records
  • Write down the questions you want answered
Do not diagnose your child from a photograph or compare one child's head shape with another child's photograph online. In India, be cautious of products marketed online for "flat head" shaping; a helmet is never a substitute for medical assessment, and using one without assessment can delay the right care.

Ask your paediatrician or other qualified healthcare professional whether specialist assessment is appropriate. If symptoms involving vision, hearing, breathing, development or other areas are present, mention them clearly.

A first sign is a reason to look carefully. It is not a diagnosis.

What to say at the appointment

Hindi-speaking families often search with symptom phrases rather than the medical term: सिर टेढ़ा (head looks crooked), सिर चपटा (head looks flat), सिर की हड्डी जल्दी जुड़ना (skull bone joining early). Whatever words you use, the same sentence works with any doctor: "I have noticed my baby's head shape changing, and I would like to know whether a specialist should look at it."
