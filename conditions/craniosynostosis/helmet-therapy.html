Helmet therapy

Helmets confuse more Indian parents than almost anything else, partly because unbranded "flat head helmets" are sold online with no assessment at all. Here is what helmets actually do, when specialists prescribe them, and what to ask.

Three rules before anything else. A helmet never treats craniosynostosis by itself: a fused suture is treated by a surgical team, and Cleveland Clinic states this plainly. A helmet is a prescribed medical device with fittings and follow-up, not a product to buy online. And if your child's team did not prescribe one, your child does not need one.

The day in a helmet

Off only for bathing and skin checks. Sweating settles after the first couple of weeks; in Indian summers, plan cooler indoor hours and lighter clothing.

Two different jobs

After endoscopic surgery for craniosynostosis

After a minimally invasive (endoscopic) operation, the helmet guides how the rapidly growing skull reshapes, and protects the head. St. Louis Children's, which developed the protocol it states is used around the world, fits the helmet as early as the day after surgery; Seattle Children's starts about two weeks after surgery. This is the only craniosynostosis situation where a helmet is part of treatment, and always alongside surgery, never instead of it.

For positional flattening (no fused suture)

For positional plagiocephaly or brachycephaly, where no suture is fused, helmets are one option among several, usually considered when repositioning has not helped. Cleveland Clinic notes the best results when therapy starts early in infancy, after about 4 months, and that starting after about 8 months often takes longer; once skull plates have fused, a helmet cannot change head shape.

Skin and heat: the part that matters more in India

  • Check the scalp every day for red or sore areas; a red mark that does not fade within about an hour of removing the helmet is a reason to call the orthotist.
  • Clean the helmet daily and let it dry fully before it goes back on (Atlanta suggests rubbing alcohol and a textured cloth).
  • Sweating is expected for the first couple of weeks and then settles; dress the baby lighter than usual, and in Indian summers plan for cooler indoor hours where possible.
  • Atlanta advises removing the helmet during fever and calling the orthotist about restarting after any break of two days or more.

Cost, and the questions that protect you

No major international site publishes a fixed price, and Indian pricing is not published in a dated, verifiable form we could confirm; ask for a written quotation. Adapt CAPPSkids' orthotist questions for India:

Ask any helmet provider

  • Is this helmet prescribed by my child's treating team, and will you share fitting reports with them?
  • How is the head shape captured: a scan, a cast or guesswork?
  • How many cranial helmets have you fitted, and how many children needed a second one?
  • What does the quoted price include: reviews, adjustments, a replacement if outgrown?
  • How often will reviews happen, and what happens if we live in another city?
  • What is the total cost in writing, and is any part covered by our insurance or scheme?
Key point
One honest disagreement, disclosed: most major centres treat endoscopic surgery plus helmet as a standard option for young infants. The Craniofacial Center in Dallas, whose director is First Signs' international content reviewer, does not use helmets and states its concern that they can restrict growth. Both positions are published by respected centres. First Signs presents both, and your child's own team, which knows your child, is the right judge between them.
