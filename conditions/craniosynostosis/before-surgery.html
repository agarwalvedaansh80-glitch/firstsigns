Before surgery: preparing your family

A planned operation can feel overwhelming even after the diagnosis is clear.

Your journey · 2 of 7
←→
Before surgery

First Signs' preparation material is designed to help families organise information, not to give medical instructions.

Before the hospital admission, families can ask the treating team

  • What should we bring?
  • What should the child eat or drink before admission?
  • Which medicines or supplements should be discussed?
  • How long should we expect to be in hospital?
  • Who will be the main surgical and paediatric contacts?
  • What should we expect immediately after surgery?
  • What signs after discharge should prompt an urgent call?
  • When is the first follow-up?
  • How will blood be arranged if it is needed, and should we arrange donors in advance?

The folder that saves you on the day

Keep a folder, physical or digital, with:

  • Referral papers
  • Scan reports and images
  • Blood-test reports if requested by the treating team
  • Medication information
  • Insurance documents, scheme cards (PM-JAY, state scheme or CGHS) and ID proofs
  • Emergency contact numbers
  • Questions for the medical team
Do not copy another family's medication, fasting, wound-care or recovery instructions. Follow the instructions given by your own treating team.

Anaesthesia, fasting and blood, explained

Three things frighten parents most before admission. Knowing how teams handle them helps.

Anaesthesia

A specialist paediatric anaesthetist keeps your child asleep, monitored and pain-free throughout, and will meet you before the operation. GOSH's practice at many centres lets a parent accompany the child to the anaesthetic room; ask whether yours allows it. Bring your questions to that pre-operative conversation; it exists for you.

Fasting

"Nil by mouth" rules exist so the stomach is empty and the anaesthetic is safe. The team will give exact times for stopping food and milk, and clear fluids often continue until closer to surgery. Follow their written times exactly, and if your child accidentally feeds, tell the team immediately rather than staying quiet; they adjust safely all the time.

Blood

GOSH's published framing is the honest one: a transfusion is sometimes required, and matched blood is kept ready either way. Ask how often this team's patients need blood for this operation, whether conservation approaches such as cell salvage or pre-operative erythropoietin are part of their practice, and whether you should arrange standby donors, which many Indian hospitals request as routine.

For families, preparation is also emotional. It is normal to feel frightened. Ask the hospital what support is available. If possible, identify one family member who can keep records and communicate updates so that the parents can focus on the child.
