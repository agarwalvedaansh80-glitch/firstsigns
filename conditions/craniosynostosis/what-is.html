What is craniosynostosis?

Craniosynostosis is a birth condition in which one or more of the sutures of a baby's skull fuse earlier than expected.

Learn · 1 of 11
←→
What is craniosynostosis?

Sutures are the joints between the skull bones. They allow the skull to expand as the brain grows. When a suture closes too early, growth of the skull is restricted in that direction and growth can be redirected through the sutures that remain open. The result can be an unusual head shape.

The condition is not one single appearance, and not every unusual head shape is craniosynostosis. The effect depends on which suture is involved, whether one or more sutures are affected, when the fusion occurred and whether there are associated features.

Craniosynostosis may be non-syndromic, meaning it occurs without a recognised syndrome, or syndromic, where it occurs as part of a broader genetic condition. Some cases have a known genetic cause; many do not.

Key point
The important message for families is simple: appearance can be the first clue, but diagnosis belongs to qualified clinicians using examination, clinical history and appropriate investigations.

If someone has told you that your baby's head shape may represent craniosynostosis, ask what the next specialist step is and what the proposed investigation is intended to establish.

How common is it?

International reviews estimate craniosynostosis affects roughly 1 in 1,700 births globally (2022 systematic review and meta-analysis figures used in our programme materials), and published Indian series indicate that children in India often reach specialists late, at an average age of around 10.9 months in one 2025 tertiary-centre series. Early assessment widens the options available, which is why awareness is the heart of First Signs.
