Types of craniosynostosis

The skull has several major sutures. Craniosynostosis is described according to which suture has fused early.

Learn · 3 of 11
←→
Types of craniosynostosis

Sagittal

The sagittal suture runs along the top of the skull from front to back. Early fusion can result in a long, narrow head shape (sometimes called scaphocephaly). It is the most common single-suture type.

Coronal (one side)

The coronal sutures run from the sides of the skull toward the top. One-sided involvement can produce asymmetry of the forehead and face. This is the type Vedaansh was born with.

Coronal (both sides)

Involvement of both coronal sutures can produce a broader, shorter head shape and is more often associated with syndromic forms.

Metopic

The metopic suture runs in the midline of the forehead. Early fusion can be associated with a triangular or narrowed forehead (sometimes called trigonocephaly).

Lambdoid

The lambdoid suture is at the back of the skull. Early fusion can produce posterior asymmetry. It is the rarest single-suture type, and the one most often confused with positional flattening.

Multi-suture and syndromic

There are also complex or multi-suture forms, and syndromic forms associated with recognised genetic conditions such as Apert, Crouzon and Pfeiffer syndromes. These need care from a multidisciplinary team.

The names can sound complicated. Families do not need to memorise them before seeing a specialist. What matters is understanding that different patterns can have different clinical considerations, and that the exact diagnosis should be established by the treating team.

Key point
If your child has been given a specific type, ask the specialist to explain which suture is involved, what that means for your child's skull growth and what monitoring or treatment is being considered.
